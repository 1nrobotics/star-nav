The same navigation stack, on two legs

Booster Robotics' T1 humanoid explores a MuJoCo house and forest with the planner the Star Nav drone and the Lite3 quadruped use. EGO-Planner plans at trunk height; the quadruped's tracker turns its setpoints into velocity commands for T1's learned walking policy; a terrain filter fed by the lidar on its head lets the planner take the ramp and walk around the furniture.

How it works

The planning side is the quadruped's: one launch file, exploration_legged.launch, serves both walking robots and sets the height, sensor mount, inflation and speed per robot. What is new is the robot: its model, its policy, and the sensors on its head.

Control

Setpoint → joint torques

  1. Tracker. The quadruped's: velocity = setpoint velocity + 1.5 × position error, turned to the planner's yaw, standing still on a resting setpoint.
  2. Walking policy. The velocity policy from Booster Robotics' booster_gym, a 47 → 256 → 128 → 128 → 12 network. Its inputs are gravity direction, body rates, the command, a 1.5 Hz gait clock, joint angles and velocities, and the last action. The clock stops on a zero command, so the robot stands.
  3. No PyTorch. The policy ships as a TorchScript file. Its weights are read straight from the archive's raw tensor files and saved as NumPy arrays; nothing from the download is unpickled or run.
  4. PD. The upstream gains, every 2 ms, clipped to the model's torque limits.
Planning

Sized for a humanoid

  1. Height. EGO-Planner plans at the trunk, 0.67 m above the floor.
  2. Sensors on the head. The lidar sits at 1.2 m and looks down to −50°, so it sees the floor close to the feet. The robot's own body is hidden from both lidar and depth camera.
  3. Terrain filter. As for the quadruped (9 cm step threshold), but anything up to 1.25 m counts: the dining table is an obstacle, not a roof.
  4. Clearance. 0.35 m inflation, as the arms reach 0.27 m from the trunk; the house walls are 1.6 m, above the head.

Not yet
  • It cannot climb the 7 cm steps; the mission goes up the ramp and down the steps.
  • Depth camera: without the terrain filter the planner may route over obstacles lower than about 1 m, which the robot cannot follow. A virtual ceiling above the walking height will fix it; use the lidar for now.
  • The upstream model's arms have no collision geoms, so a touch with an arm is not counted.
  • As for the quadruped: ground-truth pose, static worlds only, no CI (the model is downloaded), no hardware branch yet.

Quick start

Ubuntu 20.04 with ROS Noetic and the workspace built as for the drone (see the drone page). The T1 model and policy are downloaded at a pinned commit with a checksum, not committed.

01Install the humanoid

./src/scripts/setup_mujoco_env.sh --t1   # model + policy weights

02Watch it walk

roslaunch star_nav_bringup exploration_t1.launch use_rviz:=true   # house, lidar
rostopic pub -1 /exploration/trigger geometry_msgs/PoseStamped "{}"

03Headless tests

rostest star_nav_bringup t1_smoke.test                  # house, lidar
rostest star_nav_bringup t1_smoke.test world:=forest    # pillar forest
